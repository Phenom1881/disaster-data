How this record is built

The historical layer. Every 1980 to 2024 figure on this page is NOAA's, drawn from the National Centers for Environmental Information state mapping data for the U.S. Billion-Dollar Weather and Climate Disasters product: event impacts and CPI-adjusted losses per state, per hazard. NOAA retired the product in 2025; its final release counted 403 events. That data is preserved here frozen, exactly as published. Uses NOAA data but is not endorsed by or affiliated with NOAA.

The threshold going forward. A disaster enters the BDDT record if overall losses reached one billion dollars in nominal terms in the year the event occurred. There is no retroactive inflation adjustment for eligibility: a 1990 disaster is judged in 1990 dollars, a 2025 disaster in 2025 dollars. CPI-adjusted figures may appear on a record as supplemental context, never as the basis for entry. This is a deliberate divergence from NOAA's method, made so that events stay documented as they were understood at the time. The NOAA historical figures above remain CPI-adjusted because that is how NOAA published its final state data.

What counts as an impact. NOAA's file records one row per state per event. A single event touching five states is one event nationally and five state impacts here. Impact counts reflect that file, not dollars or unique events.

Provenance and status. Every record carries a prov_flag: NOAA for untouched legacy records, NOAA-REVISED where BDDT amends a legacy record, and BDDT for new independent records. Records open as Provisional while loss assessments mature and move to Final on review. Deaths are recorded in three separate fields: direct, indirect, and excess mortality, so contested estimates are never conflated with confirmed counts.

Sources. Cost figures follow a six-tier source hierarchy: federal and state assessments and reinsurance catastrophe reports rank highest, and media reporting is never a primary cost basis. Each record cites its sources with tier, publisher, and access date.

Openness. BDDT data is published under CC BY 4.0. The Billion Dollar Disaster Tracker is an independent project of DisasterData.IO and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or a product of NOAA, FEMA, or any US government agency.
