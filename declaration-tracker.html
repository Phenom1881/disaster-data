Who is still waiting on FEMA.

When a governor or tribal leader asks the President for a major disaster or emergency declaration, the request can sit for weeks. FEMA's open data only shows a request once it is decided. This tracks each one from the day it was filed to the day FEMA acted, and how long that took.

How this works

This is an unofficial reading of public records. It is not a FEMA product and FEMA has not reviewed it.

Where requests come from

FEMA's Daily Operations Briefing included a "Declaration Requests in Process" table listing every pending request, the programs asked for, and the date it was filed. DisasterData.IO archives each briefing, and this tracker reads that table out of every archived copy, then follows each request from the first day it was listed to the last.

The briefing stopped carrying the table after October 29, 2026. Requests filed since then do not appear here until FEMA acts on them.

How outcomes are decided

  • Declared: OpenFEMA shows a declaration for the same requester and type, on or after the request date, around the day the briefing marked it approved or dropped it.
  • Turned down: OpenFEMA's Declaration Denials has the same request. Denials there post in batches, often weeks after the decision.
  • Per the Ops Brief: the briefing marked it approved or denied, but OpenFEMA has no matching record yet.
  • Left the briefing: it dropped off the table and no outcome could be found.

Reading the dates

The briefing prints the requested date without a year; the year is taken from the briefing. For an appeal, the briefing sometimes shows the original request date and sometimes the appeal date, so an appeal's wait can include the original review. When the briefing changed a request's date partway through, the tracker keeps the date it showed last.

Get the data

The full ledger is updated every morning with the briefing archive: declaration-tracker.json. Each request lists the briefings it appeared in, its programs, and the OpenFEMA record it was matched to. Spot something wrong? Report a data issue.
