The federal disaster record for Lake County runs between 2003 and 2020, covering 8 declarations in completed fiscal years. Its most recent major disaster declaration was Covid-19 Pandemic in 2020. The hazards behind these declarations were most often severe storm, biological, and other. Since 2000, FEMA has obligated about $15.2 million in Public Assistance funding to the jurisdiction, with the largest category being emergency protective measures. FEMA Individual Assistance records show about $10.8 million approved through the Individuals and Households Program.

How these numbers are built

Declaration history comes from FEMA's OpenFEMA Disaster Declarations Summaries. A declaration is counted once for each designated area it names, so a single disaster covering many localities is counted in each locality. For that reason, jurisdiction counts do not sum to Ohio's statewide total. This page counts declarations that named this jurisdiction as a designated area. Headline declaration totals cover completed federal fiscal years (Oct. 1 through Sept. 30). Activity from the current fiscal year may appear as the most recent event before it is included in those totals. Risk and vulnerability context is joined at the county-equivalent level using the five-digit Census/FIPS GEOID. CDC/ATSDR SVI and FEMA National Risk Index Social Vulnerability are different measures and are not treated as interchangeable. Public Assistance, Individual Assistance, and Hazard Mitigation figures reflect the underlying federal datasets and may change as records are updated or projects close out. DisasterData.IO uses federal source data but is not endorsed by or affiliated with FEMA, CDC, or ATSDR.
