Maine: Weather Emergency Declarations

Original state weather-emergency declarations, compared with federal FEMA declarations and nearby NOAA/NWS Storm Events evidence.

Coverage: 2000-present: 2011-present Governor executive-order archives (LePage and Mills), plus storm emergency proclamations from 2013-2024 taken from the Governor's proclamations and news releases; the adapter does not yet read the Mills proclamations listing. Plus declarations added by hand from official announcements, order text and dated news coverage, including Governor Baldacci's 2004-2010 weather emergency proclamations from the Maine State Library's yearly archives of his press releases. No state declaration has been found for Governor King's years: DR-1326 (spring 2000 flooding and ice jams), EM-3164 and DR-1371 (March 2001 snow and flooding) and DR-1468 (2002-2003 winter cold and frost); the State Archives' 1975-2000 proclamation collection on Digital Maine did not return his items. Nor for EM-3174, EM-3190 and EM-3194 (2003 snowstorms), EM-3209, EM-3210 and DR-1591 (March 2005 snow), EM-3265 (December 2005), DR-1716 (Oxford County, July 2007: declared, but no date found), DR-1788 (July 2008), DR-1852 (June 2009), DR-4367 (March 2018), DR-4647 (October 2021), DR-4696 (December 2022) or DR-4719 (spring 2023); Governor Mills's request letters for the last two describe activating the State Emergency Operations Center and mention no declaration. Absence from this page must not be interpreted as absence of a state emergency.

Methodology and coverage

Federal declarations are sourced from OpenFEMA via this site's national build. State declarations are limited by the coverage statement above. NOAA proximity matches identify potentially related observed events within a configured window of each state declaration's signing date; they do not independently prove operational impacts or legal causation. NOAA's published Storm Events data used here runs through about 2026-07-30; declarations signed after that show as pending until NOAA publishes those months. The federal crosswalk match uses a wider 21 day window than the NOAA match, since a federal declaration is often filed weeks after the state action that preceded it.
